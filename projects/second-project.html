Brushless Motor Driver from Scratch

Course project, EE 101 · Fall 2025

Code

A 40 V, 15 A field-oriented-control driver, built because I wanted to understand what a commercial ESC is actually doing between the throttle command and the phase currents. This is the short version of the page — a good example of what a project writeup looks like when you don't have a formal report to attach.

How it works

Three half-bridges of 60 V N-channel MOSFETs, driven by a bootstrapped gate driver, with low-side shunts sensing two of the three phase currents. An STM32G4 runs the Clarke and Park transforms and two PI loops at 20 kHz, which happens to be the PWM frequency.

Bus voltage12–40 V
Continuous current15 A with airflow
Control loopFOC, 20 kHz
Position senseAS5047P magnetic encoder

What went wrong

  • Rev A had the current-sense amplifier inputs swapped, so the loop ran away instantly. Two dead MOSFETs and one memorable bang.
  • Gate ringing at turn-on was bad enough to false-trigger the low side; a 10 Ω gate resistor fixed it at the cost of a bit of efficiency.
  • The encoder needed a mechanical offset calibration I hadn't budgeted time for.
If the current sense polarity is wrong, FOC doesn't degrade gracefully. Check it with a bench supply current-limited to 500 mA before you connect the real bus.

Results

Smooth rotation from 5 to 4000 RPM, holding position within about 0.3 degrees under a hand load. Efficiency measured at 91% at half load, which is worse than a good commercial ESC and better than I expected.
