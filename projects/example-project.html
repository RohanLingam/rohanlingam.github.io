Wearable Biosignal Logger

Personal project · Jan – Jun 2025

Report (PDF)  /  Code  /  Schematics

This is a three-channel ECG/EMG recorder small enough to tape to your chest and cheap enough that losing one isn't a disaster. It samples at 1 kHz per channel, writes to a microSD card, and runs about 26 hours on a 400 mAh LiPo. I built it because the commercial options I could find cost more than my rent and wouldn't give me raw samples.

Why it was hard

Biopotentials are tiny — a surface ECG is roughly 1 mV peak, sitting on top of a half-cell electrode offset that can drift by hundreds of millivolts, in a room full of 50/60 Hz mains hum that couples straight into your body. The whole design is a fight between three constraints:

  • Gain vs. offset. You want a lot of gain, but DC offset saturates the front-end before you get there.
  • Common-mode rejection vs. cost. CMRR depends on how well the two input paths match, and matching costs money.
  • Power vs. noise. Every microamp you cut raises the noise floor somewhere.

Analog front-end

I ended up using a TI ADS1299-style architecture: a low-noise PGA per channel feeding a 24-bit delta-sigma converter, with the DC offset handled by AC-coupling ahead of the PGA rather than by a servo loop. A right-leg-drive amplifier pushes the inverted common-mode signal back into the body, which bought about 30 dB of hum rejection for the cost of one op-amp.

Firmware

The MCU does almost nothing on purpose: DMA moves SPI frames from the ADC into a double buffer, and a low-priority task flushes full buffers to the SD card in 512-byte blocks. Doing the write in the interrupt handler was the first version and it dropped samples every time the card decided to do internal wear leveling. 

void DMA1_Channel2_IRQHandler(void) {
    buf_ready = active;      // hand the full half to the writer
    active   ^= 1;           // keep filling the other half
    HAL_DMA_IRQHandler(&hdma_spi1_rx);
}
Lesson: an SD card is not a peripheral, it's a small computer with its own opinions about when to be busy. Buffer accordingly.

Results

Side-by-side against a borrowed clinical recorder, R-peak timing agreed within 3 ms over a 20-minute session and the QRS morphology was close enough that I couldn't tell the traces apart by eye. The noise floor is about 2x worse than the reference unit, which I think is the electrode cable rather than the board.
